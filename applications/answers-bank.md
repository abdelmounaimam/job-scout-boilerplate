# Answer bank — the questions every ATS asks

Reusable answers, written once, so that a form takes minutes and not an hour. Fill these in during setup; they are the same on almost every application.

⚠️ **Rewrite, never paste blindly.** An answer that mentions the wrong company, or reuses a phrase from another posting, is worse than a generic one.

## Compensation

⚠️ **Never give the current salary. Answer with the expectation.** The current figure anchors every later step of the negotiation, and it is the one number that cannot be taken back.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Salary expectation | the target, not the floor: the floor is the walk-away, not the opening |
| Notice period | the real one, verified in the contract, not the one people assume |
| Earliest start date | |

## Recurring free-text questions

Write each of these once, in 4 to 8 lines, then tailor per posting.

- **Why this company?** ⚠️ This is the one that has to be genuinely rewritten every time. A generic answer here reads as a mass application, which is exactly what it is.
- **Why are you leaving your current role?** Forward-looking, never a complaint.
- **Tell us about a project you are proud of.** One project, one problem, one decision, one outcome, ideally with a number.
- **What are you looking for in your next role?**
- **How did you hear about us?**

## Demographic and voluntary questions

Optional by law in most jurisdictions. Answering or declining changes nothing to the application. Decide once and be consistent.

---

## ⚠️ Two things never to do

1. **Never invent a story.** Not a project, not a metric, not a team. It survives exactly until the interview that asks a follow-up question, and it costs the whole process.
2. **Never sign an attestation on the candidate's behalf.** Some forms carry a checkbox certifying that the applicant personally completed the application, with dismissal as the stated consequence. Only the candidate can tick that one.
